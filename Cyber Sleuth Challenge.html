04 · Mythbusting

Cyber myths, debunked.

MYTH
"If it says HTTPS, it's safe."
REALITY
HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted — not that the site is legitimate. Attackers get free certificates in minutes. Look at the domain, not the lock.
MYTH
"If it's from someone I know, it's fine."
REALITY
Spoofing is everywhere. Display names, sender addresses, even phone numbers can be faked. If the request feels off, verify through a separate channel.

04 · Mythbusting (cont.)

Modern scams are everywhere.

MYTH
"PDFs can't hurt you."
REALITY
PDFs can carry malware. They can run scripts, redirect you to credential-stealing sites, and embed malicious links that look harmless until you click.
The pretexts you'll see this year:
🎁 Gift card requests
🚨 "Urgent" account alerts
💼 Fake job offers
🛣 Unpaid toll fees
🚗 DMV penalty notices
📦 Delivery rescheduling
🏦 Bank fraud "alerts"
🤖 AI deepfake voice calls
Vectors change. The playbook — urgency, authority, secrecy — does not.

07 · Other phishy tactics

Data mining.

01 of 03
Pulling valuable information out of public datasets — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Google reviews — and using it to build a believable lie.
WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE
An attacker who knows your office manager's name, your hygienist's first day, and your practice's anniversary — because you posted it.
SOURCES THEY MINE
· LinkedIn job posts
· Practice website bios
· Google reviews
· Facebook events
· Instagram tagged photos
· "Welcome Dr. X" posts
Defense: Audit what your practice posts publicly. The less attackers know, the harder it is to be specific.

07 · Other phishy tactics

Social engineering.

02 of 03
Tactics designed to earn your trust so you lower your guard — then hand over information that should never leave the office.
DEFENSE
Verify roles in person. IT won't ever ask for your password. Doctors don't grant server access by hallway conversation.
REAL THINGS PEOPLE HAVE SAID:
"I'm from IT. I need your login to fix a quick problem before Dr. Lewis sees it."
"The doctor said I could access the server while she's at lunch. Just need the password."
"This is the new auditor — I just need patient records emailed to this address by EOD."

07 · Other phishy tactics

Vishing & smishing.

03 of 03
VISHING Voice phishing
The phone call you didn't expect.
  • "IRS" demanding back taxes by gift card
  • "Bank fraud team" walking you through a transfer
  • AI-generated executive voice asking for an urgent wire
  • Fake award notifications wanting your info to "send a prize"
SMISHING SMS / text phishing
The text from "USPS" you weren't waiting on.
  • USPS / Amazon delivery scams
  • Bank fraud alerts with a link
  • Tech support "log in to fix it"
  • Job offer / DMV / toll-fee scams
Pattern recognition: urgency + authority + unknown sender = stop. Call the supposed party through a known number.

08 · Daily habits

Cyber hygiene = a clean click culture.

01
Unique passwords + password manager.
One leak shouldn't compromise everything.
02
Enable MFA on everything.
Stolen password ≠ stolen account.
03
Lock screens when away.
Win + L. Cmd + Ctrl + Q. Reflex it.
04
Don't reuse personal credentials at work.
Or work credentials anywhere personal.
05
Only download from verified sources.
Bookmarks, vendor portals — not search ads.
06
If it seems suspicious, say something.
Better to flag five non-issues than miss one real one.
